## How to Use this Book

Players will often want to explore cities as much as they want to explore the wilderness. Not only do shops off  er goods and services for the player characters, but they can also be a valuable resource in handing out quests or building story hooks.

The intention of this book is to give dungeon masters and game masters a framework for  creating shops in fantasy settings quickly while providing hooks to advance player actions. The book is broken down into two main sections.

The fi  rst section is called Shop Details.

On page 7, there is a random List of Shops. If a GM or DM just needs a location, they can roll randomly and build a shop. Also on that page are Years in Business and Number of Employees plus rules for using the following tables in the section.

The Shop Appearance tables give you words that can be combined to give you a sense of the look of the shop.

The Person Descriptors tables are used by placing a couple of words together to create a description of the owner, employees, or patrons of the shop.

The Back Room is a mini-section including two tables: Desk and Storeroom. Occasionally players get up to mischief, so these are helpful if characters begin snooping around or need to search for clues.

The Shopkeeper Rumors give dungeon masters and game masters the answers they need when their players want to chat up the owner of the shop while also providing story hooks.

The Secrets table is a bit special. This table can be used by the DM to create stories around the shop owner by giving them a tidbit of background. It could also lead to the players discovering mysteries about the shop owner.

The second section is simply called Shops.

In this section are twenty diff  erent types of shops. Each shop has naming options plus special and mundane wares. Also, DMs or GMs can assume the shops have a selection of ordinary goods if desired.  All are arranged in random tables, so they can be generated quickly.

Never tell your players there are no shops on the street. Use this resource to create a shop on the fl  y  and  populate your worlds with interesting items and non-player characters. Good luck and good shopping.

Matt Davids 4/28/2022

## Years in Business

Roll 1D100 for years of operation. If your world is  old,  roll  1D10  then  multiply the D100 result by it.

## Number of Employees

Roll 1D6-1 to determine the number of employees plus the owner.

## Shop Appearance

Use the Appearance Tables to create the look of the shop.

Roll  a  D4  twice  and  then  roll  a  D100  on  the corresponding Appeance Tables. Combine the results to imagine a unique shop.

Repeat the process for interior and exterior if desired.

## Person Descriptors

Use  the  Descriptors  Tables  to  create  the look  of    the  shop  owners,  employees,  or patrons.

Roll  a  D4  twice  and  then  roll  a  D100  on the corresponding Descriptors Tables. Combine the results to imagine a person.

## The Back Room

Roll on the Desk or the Storeroom tables, if characters explore the back of the shop.

## Shopkeeper Rumors

As characters interact with a shopkeeper, you can roll on the this table to add details into the setting.

## Secrets

To add dymanic elements to the story, roll on  this  table  to  create  secrets  about  the shop or the owner.
