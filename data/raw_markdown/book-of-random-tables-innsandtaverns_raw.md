## How to Use this Book

The purpose of this book is to help dungeon masters and game masters quickly generate inns or taverns either on the fl  y at the gaming table or during session prep. The next time your players ask what inn is nearby, you won't have to stumble around for the answer.

The book is organized in the form of twenty-fi  ve D100 random tables, so simple rolls of the dice can create interesting inns or taverns. GMs and DMs can let randomness decide key details and let an original and unique location emerge.

The random tables are arranged in such a way as to aid creation by starting with the basics and then moving into more complex concepts.

The fi  rst section is Inn or Tavern Names. This book provides 400 names so each inn or tavern you create will be distinct.

Next is the Inn or Tavern Appearance section. These tables include Exterior Appearance and Interior Appearance tables to give GMs and DMs clues about how to describe the location. Since 200 description words are included for both interior and exterior some very interesting combinations can be created.

In the section called Secret Rooms, the dungeon master can include tunnels or chambers that increase intrigue and player engagement.

The People section contains a number of random tables meant to help GMs populate their new inn or tavern. Bards are always performing in the common room, so roll on the table to see who is entertaining the patrons. The Owners, Patrons, and Servers tables round out the section.

The Meals section provides a realistic middle age or fantasy meal. There is always at least one player who wants to know what food or drink the tavern is serving. Now DMs have that information at their fi  ngertips.

Players  usually  have  their  characters  search  every  room  or  location.  The  Items  in  Inn Rooms Section solves the problem of coming up with ideas out of thin air.

The  Inn  Encounters  section  gives  game  masters  and  dungeon  masters  200  diff  erent situations that can be happening in the inn or tavern. Keep your players on their toes and constantly engaged with the setting.

Adventurers need to blow off   steam every once in a while, so that's why there are carousing tables. In the Carousing section, you will fi  nd 200 funny, weird, and wacky situations the characters have gotten themselves into by partying hard.

Inns and taverns are where the town gossip is spread. The Rumors and Odd Jobs section is where game masters can pepper in interesting details and create the appearance of a fully active and living world.

The sparks of creativity in this book give dungeon masters the ability to fi  re their own imaginations, so there is never a dull or boring inn or tavern again. Simply roll the dice to hear the murmur of the common room and the melody of the bard.
